A **hypercycle** is a ring of chemicals where each one is a *catalyst* for the replication of the next: chemical 1 speeds up the copying of 2, 2 of 3, and so on until 5 speeds up 1. [Eigen and Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercycle_(chemistry)) proposed it as a way for the first self-copying molecules to coexist: rival replicators crowd each other out, but in a ring every one needs the catalyst before it, so none can win alone.

Each cell holds a concentration of each of the five chemicals (one color each, added together) that totals 1. The sheet starts empty except for a random mixture in its middle quarter, and the reaction spreads outward from there as the chemicals diffuse. Across the surface the concentrations chase each other in spiral waves.

### What to try
- **catalysis by the previous chemical** is the ring. Set it to 0 and the waves wash out: with no catalysis the five just compete.
- **uncatalyzed replication** is how fast each is copied without help; **diffusion** moves chemicals into neighboring cells.
- **step size** and **small steps per tick** only change how fast it runs.
- Press **Reset** to start from a new random mixture. The charts show how much of the sheet each chemical holds on average.
