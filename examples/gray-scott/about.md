Two imaginary chemicals, **u** and **v**, share a sheet, and three things happen to them. The **reaction** turns u into more v wherever v is present (u + 2v → 3v). **Diffusion** spreads each chemical out into its neighbors, u faster than v. **Decay** breaks v down, while u is supplied everywhere to replace what the reaction uses up. Together that grows spots, stripes, mazes and coral: this is the [Gray-Scott model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction%E2%80%93diffusion_system), a classic of pattern formation (Turing's idea of how animals get their markings).

### What to try
- Press the **Pattern** buttons: each sets *supply* and *decay* to a known setting.
- **supply** is how fast u is topped up; **decay** is how fast v breaks down. Small changes flip the pattern from spots to stripes.
- **diffusion of u / v** sets how fast each spreads out, and so how fine the pattern is.
- **Palette** only changes colors. The color shows how much v is in each cell.
- *starting blobs* and the seed only take effect after **Reset**.
